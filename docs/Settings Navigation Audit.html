Settings Navigation Audit

A gym owner told us he has to guess his way around Settings. This report traces the likely causes in the product, compares Settings in 14 products that gym owners use or that do this well, and proposes a new structure and a plan.

ScopeWeb app Settings: 17 sections, the ways into it, and settings that live elsewhere
EvidenceCode on main, screenshots and measurements from a dev build, vendor help centres, Mobbin captures
Not yet doneWatching the owner use it. A 15-minute test is the first step of the plan
The short answer

Five things in Settings make an owner guess

We can only confirm what tripped him up by watching him. The product still gives five strong candidates, and any one of them is enough to make someone guess. They are ranked by how many everyday tasks they get in the way of.

  1. On a phone, Settings is a sideways strip of 17 buttons with no headings P1

    Only “Overview”, “Your profile” and half of “Login & security” fit on the screen. Membership plans is four and a half screens of swiping away. Team members is six and a half.

  2. The same button opens different pages, and three buttons open the wrong one P1

    “Settings” in the sidebar opens Overview. “Settings” in the profile menu opens WhatsApp. On Business, the buttons for Connect Razorpay, Open payment settings and Finish invoice setup point to a Settings page that doesn't exist, so the owner lands on Overview.

  3. Some settings live where an owner would never look P1

    Trainers are inside Products & services. Expense categories are inside Payments, whose heading says “Choose how members can pay you”. Renewal reminders live in Settings › Automated messages, but the menu item called Automations doesn't have them. Adding a branch happens in the sidebar branch menu, not in Settings › Branches.

  4. Headings overlap and some words mean two things P2

    “Business setup” and “Your gym” both describe the gym, and the main menu also has “Business”. The heading “Account” means you, not the gym. The Team members card counts “3 members” in an app where Members means paying customers. “Payments” is a Settings page, a Business tab and a Members tab, and each one means something different.

  5. The Settings home page is incomplete, and nothing is searchable P2

    Overview shows 10 of the 17 sections, in a different order from the side menu. It leaves out Membership plans and Automated messages, two of the most-used settings in a renewal-first product. There is no box where he can type “UPI” or “trainer” and jump there.

What we recommend

  • Before building anything (30 minutes): run a “where would you tap?” test with this owner and two others, so we know what confused him. The script is in the 15-minute test.
  • Now (about a day): fix the broken buttons and the second Settings door, complete the Overview, fix the clashing words, and add links between related pages.
  • Next (about a week): regroup Settings around what an owner manages: Your gym, Plans & pricing, WhatsApp, Payments & expenses, Team, Enquiries, then Personal. On phones, replace the strip with a grouped list you tap into, and add search. Then repeat the test.

Diagnosis

Findings

Ranked by how much they get in the way. P1 means an owner would call us about it, P2 means he can work around it but it costs time, and P3 is polish. File references point to the current code.

F1

Phone users get a 17-button sideways strip with no headings

P1
What happens
Below 1024px the grouped side menu turns into one horizontal row. The five headings are hidden, and the personal items come first. A phone shows 2½ of the 17 buttons, and no gym setting is visible without swiping.
Evidence
settings-rail.tsx:91 hides the headings with hidden … lg:block, and :76 makes the row overflow-x-auto. Measured strip: 2,618px wide in a 358px window.
Why it matters
Nielsen Norman Group on scrolling tab bars: “Out of sight is out of mind”. When the options differ from each other, people don't think to scroll for the hidden ones. For more than 15 subcategories on mobile, NN/g recommends a landing page that lists them all. When Mindbody moved its main menu from a top bar to a vertical side menu, it cited research that people scan vertical lists faster than horizontal ones. Shopify's and OpenPhone's phone apps both use a grouped vertical list.
Fix
On phones, make Settings a grouped vertical list. Tapping a row opens that section with a back arrow, and the strip goes away.
F2

Three “fix it” buttons land on the wrong page

P1
What happens
Connect Razorpay, Open payment settings and Finish invoice setup all link to /settings?tab=payments. The Payments page's real id is deals, so the unknown value falls back to Overview. Invoice details aren't in Payments anyway. They're in Business details.
Evidence
payment-link-actions.tsx:73, 95, invoice-document-actions.tsx:126. settings-sections.ts:197–198 only redirects tags and custom-fields. The tests check for the broken link text (payment-link-actions.test.tsx:267, 284, invoice-document-actions.test.tsx:361), so they pass anyway. The links came in with commit 9ab33d28.
Why it matters
The owner did what we told him and landed on a grid of cards. That is exactly “guessing the intended navigation”.
Fix
Redirect payments to the Payments page. Point Finish invoice setup at Business details › Invoice details. Add one test that checks every /settings?tab=… link in the code opens a real section.
F3

Two buttons called “Settings” open different pages

P1
What happens
The sidebar's Settings opens Overview. The profile menu's Settings opens the WhatsApp page.
Evidence
sidebar.tsx:124 links to /settings. sidebar.tsx:721 links to /settings?tab=whatsapp.
Why it matters
When the same word leads to different places, people stop trusting the words and start guessing (Nielsen heuristic 4, consistency).
Fix
Point the profile menu's Settings at the Settings home. That menu can also list the personal pages (Your profile, Login & security, Appearance), as Shopify and Linear do.

F5

Headings overlap and some words mean two things

P2
What happens
  • Business setup and Your gym both describe the gym. The gym's name sits under one and its branches under the other, and the main menu's Business means money.
  • Account means you: your profile, login and look. Square, Shopify and OpenPhone use “account” for the business, or say “Personal” or “Your account” for the person.
  • The Team members card says “3 members · 1 pending invite”. Everywhere else in UsefulDesk, members are paying customers.
  • Payments is three different things: how members pay you (Settings), the payment ledger (Business) and dues (Members).
  • Automated messages (Settings) and Automations (menu) sound like one feature but are two.
  • The Business details card says “Gym name, branches, and invoice details”, while Branches is a separate item under another heading.
Evidence
settings-sections.ts:175–185 (headings), settings-overview.tsx:187, 220–222 (card text). docs/ux-copy.md rule 10: “Same thing, same word, everywhere.”
Fix
Rename the headings (see the proposal). Count staff as “3 people”. Call the Settings page “Payment methods”. Link Automations and Automated messages to each other now, and decide on one name later.
F6

Overview is an incomplete copy of the menu, in a different order

P2
What happens
Overview has 10 cards for 17 sections. It leaves out Membership plans, Automated messages, Products & services, Login & security and API keys. The profile card at the top isn't clickable. The order doesn't match the side menu, and the WhatsApp card uses a plug icon where the menu uses the WhatsApp logo.
Evidence
settings-overview.tsx:179–265 (card list), :270–297 (profile card, not a button).
Fix
Make the home page list every section, under the same headings and in the same order as the menu, with one status line each. Put “Needs attention” at the top when something is broken.
F7

No search

P2
What happens
There's no way to type “UPI”, “trainer” or “logo” and jump to the right place. The app's global search component exists but isn't used on any page.
Evidence
components/layout/global-search.tsx is never imported. Mindbody lists its settings search bar first among three ways to find a setting. Shopify, Zoho, Deel and Plain all put search at the top of Settings.
Fix
Add a search box at the top of the Settings home and side menu, and teach it the owner's words: “package”, “PT fee”, “QR”, “staff”, “GST”, “dark mode”.
F8

On a laptop, the side menu runs past the bottom of the screen

P2
What happens
The menu is 847px tall. On a 1366×768 laptop, 7 of 17 items start below the fold. On long pages the menu sticks to the top, so Regional settings, Branches, Team members and API keys stay hidden until he reaches the end of the page.
Evidence
Measured in the dev build. settings-rail.tsx:78 uses lg:sticky lg:top-0 lg:overflow-visible.
Fix
Use fewer, tighter headings, and let the menu scroll inside its own column, capped at the screen height.
F9

Staff see every admin section

P3
What happens
Staff and View-only users see all 17 items but can only change Your profile, Login & security and Appearance. Everything else opens as View only.
Fix
For Staff and View only, show Personal first and fold the gym settings under “Managed by your owner”. Mindbody hides or greys out settings based on permissions.

What's working, and should stay

Every section has its own linkOld links are redirected, and Setup steps and error messages send owners straight to a section. The pattern is right; only some targets are wrong.
Help text is plain and says who it affects“Only this branch” and “Shared by every branch” make the gym and branch split clear. The 23 September copy rewrite shows.
Status on the cards“Connected for this branch” and “waiting for WhatsApp approval” turn Settings into a checklist. Every card should say its state.
Blocked actions explain themselvesThe “why can't I do this” popovers point at the fix. Once their links are right, they're the best route into Settings.

Recommendation

Proposed structure

  1. The gym's settings come first. The owner's own settings come last.
  2. Headings are named after what the owner manages, in his words.
  3. Each heading has at most four items, and every setting has exactly one home.
  4. A phone gets a list you tap into and back out of, never a sideways strip.
  5. Every row says its state: Connected, 2 waiting for approval, Not set up.
  6. Related pages link to each other, starting from wherever the work happens.
  7. Search understands the owner's words: package, PT fee, UPI, QR, staff.

After “Your gym”, the headings follow the product's daily loop: plans decide who is expiring, WhatsApp reminds them, payments collect, and the team follows up.

Plan

Plan

    Find out what confused him

    Later

    With the features that need it

How this was done, and its limits

  • We haven't seen what this owner struggled with. The causes are ranked by evidence and by how many everyday tasks they touch. The test in Phase 0 confirms or corrects the ranking.
  • Competitor menus come from public help centres and Mobbin captures, not signed-in trials. Vendors rename menus often, so treat the benchmark table as a snapshot from September 2026.
  • The screenshots and measurements come from a temporary dev-only page that rendered the real Settings components without a signed-in account. It has since been deleted.
  • The usability score is one reviewer's heuristic review in a single pass, without independent reviewers. Use it as a before-and-after baseline, not as a comparison with other products.
  • File references point to main at a90ef33b. The uncommitted subscriptions work in the same checkout wasn't touched.
